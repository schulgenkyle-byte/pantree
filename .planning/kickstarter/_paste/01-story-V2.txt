A murder mystery in your kitchen. Eight phones. One night you'll talk about for a year.

It is 1924. The theatre critic at the Algonquin is dead.

The Round Table is seated. The cocktails are mixed. The first course is on the table. Each guest at your dinner is holding a phone, and each phone has just lit up with a different name, a different role, and a single secret they alone know.

You tap Begin.

For the next three hours, Speakeater runs the night across all eight phones at once. Twists arrive privately. Clues cascade publicly. Each player drives part of the story by the choices they make. The reveal lands at the last cocktail.

You don't host the mystery. The app does. You pour the drinks.

What you're getting

Speakeater is a native Android app, on Google Play June 10, 2026. It is three things in one:

A multiplayer party game built on the cocktail-history record from 1862 to 1923. Five Mystery Nights ready at launch, each one written like an HBO episode: cast, hook, multiple-choice action trees, plot twists, and the reveal.

The drinks and food underneath the game. Five cocktails. Six small plates. A host timeline from the day before through zero hour. A shopping list grouped by aisle. A music note. The cocktail and recipe sides of the app, already live. 2,846 cocktails hand-transcribed from American bartender's manuscripts. 6,539 drinks total. 23,743 food recipes ranked by what you already own when you photograph the fridge.

How a Mystery Night plays out

You and seven guests are at the dinner table.

You open Speakeater, tap Host a Game, and a four-letter code appears on your phone. RYAS. Or LION. The seven other guests open Speakeater, tap Join a Game, type the code. Their phones light up with a sepia portrait, a name, a role, and the one secret only they know.

Some beats during the night are broadcast. Everyone sees them. Some beats are private. Only one player's phone lights up with a twist or a multiple-choice action. What that one player chooses changes what the others see five minutes later.

The reveal arrives at the final cocktail. The game ends. The story belongs to your table.

The Vanishing Socialite. West Egg, 1926. A young woman left the ballroom at midnight. She never made it home.

Each ships with five characters, a host playbook, a beat sheet, and the cocktail and food menu underneath. None of them is fiction I made up. The bars, the bartenders, and the drinks are pulled from primary sources you can verify on Internet Archive.

Why I built this

I'm Kyle. Solo developer. Huntsville, Alabama.

I photographed every American bartender's manuscript from 1862 to 1923. I transcribed 2,846 cocktails by hand from those photographs. I built the native Android app, the Cloudflare Workers backend, and the Anthropic Claude vision pipeline that turns one photograph of a fridge into a working pantry list.

The Mystery Nights came after. Hosting a dinner party is the most stressful thing most people do socially. The night runs on your nerves, not the food. Speakeater is the version of that night where the app carries the host load, the drinks are period-correct, and the conversation is interrupted every fifteen minutes by your phone telling you what to say next.

No co-founder. No investors. This campaign is the financing.
