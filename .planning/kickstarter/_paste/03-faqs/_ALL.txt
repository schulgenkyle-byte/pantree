[FAQ 2]

QUESTION:
How does the multiplayer game work, mechanically?

ANSWER:
Eight phones at a dinner. One host opens a game in Speakeater and gets a four-letter code. The other guests open Speakeater on their own phones, tap "Join a Game," type the code. Each phone gets a private character: name, role, the one secret only they know. The host taps Begin. Over the next three hours, the game runs across all eight phones via WebSocket. Some beats are broadcast (everyone sees them). Some beats are private (only one phone). Multiple-choice actions cascade. What one player picks changes what others see five minutes later. The reveal lands at the last cocktail.

[FAQ 3]

QUESTION:
Why Prohibition?

ANSWER:
Because the thread stops there. 1862 to 1923 is when the American bartender's manuscripts were written. 1920 to 1933 erased the bars and most of the bartenders. The craft revival of the late 1990s and 2000s was, in important respects, a project of returning to that thread and continuing it. Speakeater's job is to make the source material legible to a person at home tonight.

[FAQ 4]

QUESTION:
What is Curate-a-Party, mechanically?

ANSWER:
A menu is five drinks, six small plates, a host timeline, a shopping list, and a music note. Each menu is dated, sourced, and named for the bar that inspired it. Every Mystery Night ships with one of these menus baked in. Beyond the five Mystery Nights, fifty additional standalone Curate-a-Party menus ship across the next twelve months.

[FAQ 5]

QUESTION:
What happens to my games and menus if Speakeater shuts down?

ANSWER:
Every menu exports as a PDF. Every Mystery Night exports as a printable host-and-player kit. If the servers die, your saved games survive on your phone. Founder Rate subscriptions are honored for the operational lifetime of the app. Backers at the $30 tier or above lock the $30 a year rate forever, grandfathered as long as the subscription stays active. If the app shuts down, all Pro features unlock for everyone via the final update.

[FAQ 8]

QUESTION:
Is this an AI-generated app?

ANSWER:
No. The cocktail recipes are transcribed from primary-source manuscripts by hand. The food recipes are licensed, edited, and ranked. The Mystery Night writing is mine. The only AI involvement is the vision OCR that reads your fridge, the matching engine that ranks recipes by ingredient overlap, and the optional Video Clue Pack stretch goal which uses AI video generation tools to produce period-styled twist clips. The recipes, the games, and the cocktail history themselves are real.
