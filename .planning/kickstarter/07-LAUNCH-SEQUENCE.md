# Launch Sequence — Day-by-Day Playbook

The 30-day campaign window broken down into the tactical actions Kyle takes each day. Designed so a solo dev can run the whole thing without burning out.

---

## Pre-launch (Day -14 to Day -1)

**Day -14:** Strategy locked. This doc set committed. speakeater.com/kickstarter live with the email-capture form. PDF of the Bee's Knees menu generated and stored in R2.

**Day -13 to -10:** Hero video produced (Path A live shoot OR Path B Remotion render). Final cut by end of Day -10.

**Day -9:** Kickstarter project page drafted in Kickstarter's editor. All copy from `01-CAMPAIGN-PAGE.md` pasted in. Reward tiers configured. Video uploaded. Hero image uploaded.

**Day -8 to -5:** Influencer outreach round 1 (Tier 1 targets, 10 sends). Email 1 to anyone who's signed up so far (this is the welcome).

**Day -4:** Email 2 (the story behind the app) sent to the list.

**Day -3:** Kickstarter project page submitted for review. Allow 24-72 hours.

**Day -2:** Email 4 ("two days out") sent. Final check on payment processor wiring (Stripe via Kickstarter Backerkit). Social media bio updated with countdown.

**Day -1:** Project approved. Final eyeball pass. Phone tested by 3 trusted friends. Sleep early.

---

## Launch week (Day 0 to Day 7)

**Day 0 (LAUNCH MORNING):**

T-2 hours: Coffee. Last walk-through of the project page.

T-0 (launch goes live): Email 5 ("the link") fires automatically.

T+15min: Personal text messages to closest 20 people in your network. Plain language: "Hey, my Kickstarter just went live. Here's the link if you want to be the first to back it." No mass post yet.

T+1 hour: First social post. Instagram + Twitter + TikTok. Founder-voice. Photo of you, phone in hand, app open. "Speakeater is live on Kickstarter. The first hour is the one that matters most."

T+4 hours: Check progress. Reply to every comment and DM personally. If goal is below $500, surface the urgency in a second post.

T+12 hours: End-of-day update post. Whatever number you're at, post it honestly. "Day 1 close: $X funded / Y backers. Thank you."

**Day 1-3 (the momentum window):**

The data is clear on Kickstarter: campaigns that get to 30% of goal in the first 72 hours are the ones that fund. $4,500 in 72 hours is the floor.

- Daily founder-voice update post on Instagram + Twitter
- One TikTok / Reel daily (60-second behind-the-build clip from the b-roll library)
- Reply personally to every backer with a 1-2 sentence thank-you DM
- Influencer round 2: chase any Tier 1 commitments who haven't posted yet. Politely.

**Day 4:** First major outreach milestone. If you're above 30% of goal, send a Kickstarter "Update" message (these go to every backer's email). Title: "We hit 30% of goal in 96 hours." Body: thanks + first stretch goal tease.

**Day 5-6:** Continue daily posting. Lean into the cocktail-history content — those posts have the highest organic reach.

**Day 7:** Week 1 close. Public post: "Week 1 close: $X funded, Y backers. Three weeks to go." Honest. No hype.

---

## Maintenance (Day 8 to Day 22)

The middle of a Kickstarter is where most campaigns die. Backers from your warm network have all converted. The campaign needs new audiences daily.

**Daily tasks (every day in this window):**
- One social post on each of Instagram, Twitter, TikTok (15 min total time)
- Reply to every comment / DM (10-30 min depending on volume)
- One outreach email to a new influencer or cocktail-history account (15 min)
- Watch the funding-pace number. If pace projects under $15k, intensify.

**Day 10:** Send Kickstarter Update #2. Topic: a behind-the-scenes look at writing one of the menus. Use the Bee's Knees menu as the example. Show the historical research process. Photos of the actual cocktail books on your shelf.

**Day 15 (mid-campaign):**

Send Kickstarter Update #3. Topic: the build progress. Real screenshots of the Curate-a-Party feature being assembled. Backers love knowing their money is being spent on real work. Include a one-week-ahead roadmap: "Next week I'll show you the host timeline UI in action."

If funding is below 50% of goal at this point, you need to do something different. Options in priority order:
1. A press release to cocktail-history-specific publications (Tales of the Cocktail Foundation newsletter, Imbibe Magazine, PUNCH).
2. A paid Instagram ad targeting Wondrich-book readers and prohibition-aesthetic fans. Budget: $200 over 5 days. Only if you're below 40% of goal at day 15.
3. An "ambassador" reward: $50 backers who refer 5+ new backers get bumped to the $99 Founding Member tier. Use Backerkit referral tracking.

**Day 17-20:** Steady. One post per day. One DM round per day. Don't burn out — the final week is the hardest.

**Day 21:** Push notification or email blast: "9 days left. The Speakeasy Host Kit ($250 tier) has X spots left." Scarcity, but honestly stated.

---

## Final push (Day 23 to Day 30)

The Kickstarter algorithm shows your campaign more prominently in the "Ending Soon" feed during the last 48 hours. Use it.

**Day 23-26:** Daily founder-voice posts. The theme is honest urgency: "Five days left. We're at $Z. If we hit $Y by Friday the World Tour stretch unlocks."

**Day 27 (Update #4):** Recap of the journey so far. What's been built since the campaign launched. Show actual code commits, actual screenshots of the in-progress feature. Backers want to see momentum on the underlying work, not just marketing motion.

**Day 28-29 (final 48 hours):** Hourly posts on Instagram Stories. One post per day on the main feed. Email blast at the 48-hour mark. Email blast at the 24-hour mark.

**Day 30 (final day):**

T-12 hours: Final Kickstarter Update. Honest tally. "We have 12 hours and $X to go. Whatever happens, thank you."

T-6 hours: One last social blast across all channels. The final ask.

T-1 hour: Tag your closest 5 friends on Instagram Stories with screenshots of the campaign. Let them know it's closing.

T-0 (campaign closes): Final post. Whatever the result. Honest. "Funded at $X with Y backers. Here's what happens next."

---

## Post-campaign (Day 31 onward)

**Day 31:** Send the post-mortem Kickstarter Update. Final numbers. What unlocked. What didn't. The schedule for fulfilling rewards. Set the expectation: "I'll send you a monthly update from now until every reward has shipped."

**Day 32-45:** Build out the Curate-a-Party feature. Ship the first 5 menus into the app. Onboard the $25-and-above backers into Play Console Internal Testing.

**Day 60:** First batch of menus live in-app. Backers notified. Founder voice email goes out.

**Months 2-5:** 10 menus per month cadence. Monthly Kickstarter Update with the new menus. Monthly social post showing the production.

**Month 6:** Play Store launch. The Kickstarter backers get a 48-hour heads-up + a "founding members" badge in the Founders screen.

---

## Failure protocols

**If $0 in the first 24 hours:** something is broken (link, page, payment). Diagnose immediately. Do not wait.

**If $1k in the first 72 hours (under 7% of goal):** the campaign is in serious trouble. Options:
- Pull the campaign (Kickstarter allows this with full refunds). Better than failing publicly.
- Pivot the messaging. The story isn't landing. Try a different opening framing in your social posts.

**If under 50% of goal by day 20:** consider canceling. Better to cancel than to fail. A canceled campaign can relaunch in 6 months with the lessons learned. A failed campaign is harder to come back from.

**If the funding goal is hit but no stretch goals unlock:** that's a win. Don't apologize for not unlocking $25k. Hitting goal is the success criterion.

---

## Mental health protocol

Solo dev running a Kickstarter is intense. Real risks:

- Anxiety about checking the funding number every 5 minutes
- Resentment toward backers who pledge low tiers
- Burnout from the daily-posting cadence

Discipline:
- Check the funding number twice a day. Morning and night. Set everything else on autopilot.
- Every backer is a win. The $1 backer is just as much a part of the campaign as the $1,000 backer.
- Block 4pm-6pm every day for non-Kickstarter work (or non-work). The campaign will be there at 6pm.

The 30 days will go faster than you think. Plan a one-week recovery break starting Day 31.
