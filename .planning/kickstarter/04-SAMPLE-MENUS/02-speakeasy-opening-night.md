# Speakeasy Opening Night

**Source:** Albert Stevens Crockett, *Old Waldorf Bar Days*, 1931. Manhattan speakeasy registers, 1923 through 1933.
**Setting:** Manhattan, 1924. Below-street. The bar that opens at nine.
**Guests:** 6. Four hours. 9pm to 1am.

---

## The drinks

**Hanky Panky.** Ada Coleman, American Bar at the Savoy, London, 1903. The first widely-credited cocktail attributed to a woman bartender. By 1923 the recipe had crossed the Atlantic and was running in every Manhattan speakeasy that kept Fernet on the back shelf.

One and a half ounces London Dry. One and a half ounces sweet vermouth. Two dashes Fernet-Branca. Stir with ice. Strain into a coupe. Orange peel expressed and dropped.

**Sidecar.** Harry MacElhone, Harry's New York Bar, Paris, 1921. Cognac was a hard import in the dry American market and stayed dear after Repeal. The sugar-rimmed coupe is optional and era-correct.

One and a half ounces cognac. Three-quarter ounce Cointreau or orange curaçao. Three-quarter ounce lemon. Shake hard with ice. Strain into a coupe. Lemon peel.

**Mary Pickford.** Hotel Nacional, Havana, attributed to Fred Kaufman, 1922. Named for the silent-film actress who wintered in Cuba during the dry years. Havana was three days by steamer from New York. The rum was legal there. The 1920s American passport solved the rest.

One and a half ounces white rum. One and a half ounces pineapple juice. Six dashes grenadine. Two dashes maraschino. Shake hard with ice. Strain into a coupe. Maraschino cherry.

**Aviation.** Hugo Ensslin, Hotel Wallick, New York, 1916. The drink with crème de violette in it. By 1924 the violette had become hard to find in Manhattan, and it stayed hard to find in America until 2007. If you have the bottle, the drink is itself. If you do not, the version without violette is the Aviation No. 2 that Craddock printed in the 1930 *Savoy*.

Two ounces gin. Half ounce maraschino. Quarter ounce crème de violette. Half ounce lemon. Shake with ice. Strain into a coupe. No garnish.

**Manhattan.** The Manhattan Club, New York, 1874. The drink that named itself for the city. Originally one-to-one whiskey-to-vermouth. The two-to-one ratio came later, after taste shifted and the rye got better.

Two ounces rye. One ounce sweet vermouth. Two dashes Angostura. Stir with ice. Strain into a coupe. Brandied cherry.

## The food

The bar food of a 1924 speakeasy was anchored. Plates were salty, served cold or at room temperature, and built to outlast a four-hour night. Nothing here is plated to be photographed. It is plated to be eaten with one hand while a coupe is in the other.

**Oyster crackers in smoked paprika butter.** Soft butter, smoked paprika, sea salt, a touch of lemon zest. Smear thinly on oyster crackers. Bake at 300°F for eight minutes, until just crisp.

**Country ham on warm biscuits.** Smithfield-style salt-cured ham, sliced paper-thin. Split warm buttermilk biscuits. Grain mustard. Two per guest.

**Pickled deviled eggs.** Hard-boil. Peel. Steep the whites in beet brine for twenty-four hours. They turn ruby red and visually carry the speakeasy table. Standard deviled-egg filling.

**Roasted nuts.** Walnuts and almonds tossed in melted butter with chopped rosemary and flaky salt. Roast twelve minutes at 350°F. The bowl that disappears first.

**Cheese.** Aged cheddar, Stilton, Camembert. Quince paste. Walnut bread, sliced.

**Chocolate truffles.** Plain dark-chocolate ganache rolled in cocoa powder. Two per guest. Plate at the end of the night with espresso.

## Host timeline

The day before:
- Brine the egg whites in beet brine. Cover. Refrigerate.
- Roll the chocolate truffles. Cocoa-dust. Refrigerate.

Three hours before guests arrive:
- Bake the biscuits. Cool covered on the counter.
- Slice the ham paper-thin or unwrap the bought slices.

Ninety minutes before:
- Roast the nuts. Cool.
- Mix the paprika butter. Smear the crackers. Bake.
- Assemble the cheese plate. Slice the walnut bread.

Sixty minutes before:
- Finish the deviled-egg filling. Stuff the whites. Cover and refrigerate.
- Set up the bar: ice, glasses, jigger, strainer, mixing glass, bar spoon. Pre-chill three coupes.

Thirty minutes before:
- Stir one Hanky Panky for yourself. Taste it. Adjust the room.
- Light the candles. Cue the music.

Zero:
- Three knocks at the door. The first round is Hanky Panky for the bold, Mary Pickford for the curious, Manhattan for the regulars.

Stride piano and early hot jazz. Fletcher Henderson's New York sides. The Coon-Sanders Original Nighthawks. Bessie Smith on Columbia. 90 to 110 BPM, slow enough to hold a conversation over.

Roughly $145 for the perishables. The spirits, vermouth, and bitters are the pantry investments that survive across nights.

## Faithful and modern-flexible

The Aviation may omit the crème de violette and become the Aviation No. 2. The pickled deviled eggs simplify to standard deviled eggs when twenty-four hours of beet brine is not on the table. The Mary Pickford has a faithful Prohibition-era mocktail in the temperance set: the Florida Cooler, pineapple over grenadine over lime, finished with soda, a 1923 American hotel-bar standard. All other cocktails on this menu have direct mocktail substitutes in the app.
