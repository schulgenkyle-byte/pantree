# The Bee's Knees Garden Party

**Source:** Frank Meier, *The Artistry of Mixing Drinks*, Ritz Paris Bar, 1929.
**Setting:** Long Island estate, summer afternoon.
**Guests:** 8 to 10. Three hours. 2pm to 5pm.

---

## The drinks

**Bee's Knees.** Frank Meier, Ritz Paris, 1929. Honey syrup was the workaround for the rough gin that came over from England in glass-lined teapots. Real gin returned in 1933 and the recipe never got revised. The honey works on the good stuff too.

Two ounces London Dry. Three-quarter ounce honey syrup, one-to-one with hot water and cooled. Three-quarter ounce lemon. Shake hard. Strain into a coupe. No garnish.

**Southside.** The 21 Club, New York, opened in 1922 as a speakeasy on West 52nd Street. Mint and lime over gin, the bar's house build. The drink that signaled what kind of room you were in.

Two ounces gin. Three-quarter ounce lime. Three-quarter ounce simple. Six mint leaves. Muddle. Shake. Strain into a coupe. One mint sprig on top.

**Pimm's Cup Number One.** London, 1840s base. Adopted by New York hotel bars by 1924. The lowest-proof drink on this menu and the one your guests will keep ordering.

Two ounces Pimm's Number One. Three ounces ginger ale. Cucumber wheel, orange slice, mint sprig. Build in a highball over ice.

**Clover Club.** Philadelphia bar of the same name, 1911. Pre-Prohibition holdover that stayed in print through the dry years because the recipe is in *The Savoy Cocktail Book* (1930). Egg white over raspberry over gin.

One and a half ounces gin. Half ounce raspberry syrup. Half ounce lemon. One egg white. Dry shake without ice. Wet shake with. Strain into a coupe. Three raspberries.

**Lillet Spritz.** Faithful 1920s aperitif. The drink for guests who arrive cautious and want something they can sip for an hour.

Two ounces Lillet Blanc. Three ounces soda. Large ice. Orange twist.

## The food

The era's hotel bars served small plates that did not compete with the drinks. The list is short on purpose. Nothing here is plated. Everything stays at room temperature, on the table, while the room moves around it.

**Deviled eggs.** Hellmann's mayonnaise reached supermarket shelves in 1922. The era's deviled egg is heavier on mustard, lighter on the modern paprika dust. Twelve halves for eight guests.

**Cucumber sandwiches.** White bread, salted butter, sliced cucumber, crusts off. The English drawing-room hit that crossed the Atlantic with the same social wave that built the Long Island estates.

**Smoked salmon on rye toasts.** Toast points cut into triangles. Cream cheese, smoked salmon, capers, dill. Plate cold.

**Strawberries with crème fraîche.** One bowl in the center of the table. Whole strawberries, hulled. A ramekin of crème fraîche dusted with brown sugar. Spoons.

**Salted radishes with butter.** The French side dish that 1920s American hostesses copied from European travels. French breakfast radishes halved, European cultured butter, flaky sea salt.

**Lemon shortbread.** One-bite squares. Bake the morning of. Leave on the counter. The dessert that does not fight the cocktails.

## Host timeline

Three hours before guests arrive:
- Bake the shortbread. Cool.
- Make honey syrup. Cool.

Ninety minutes before:
- Mince mint for the Southsides. Keep cold under a damp paper towel.
- Slice cucumber thin.
- Toast and cut rye triangles.
- Whip egg yolks for the deviled eggs.

Sixty minutes before:
- Assemble deviled eggs. Cover and refrigerate.
- Halve and salt radishes.
- Slice strawberries.
- Build sandwiches. Refrigerate under a damp towel.

Thirty minutes before:
- Pour a Lillet for yourself.
- Cue music.
- Bring food platters out.

Zero:
- Guests arrive. First formal cocktail is the Bee's Knees, shaken to order. The rest follow.

## Music

Solo piano. Eubie Blake. Earl Hines's earliest stride records. Charleston Chasers when you want a touch more brass. 90 to 120 BPM, the same tempo the bartender keeps with the shaker.

The in-app playlist titled "Long Island, 1928" loads when you open the menu.

## Shopping list

For eight guests.

London Dry gin (1 × 750ml). Pimm's Number One (1 × 750ml). Lillet Blanc (1 × 750ml). Honey (4oz). Lemons (8). Limes (4). Mint (1 bunch). Raspberries (1 pint). Cucumber (2). Eggs (13). White bread (1 loaf). Rye bread (1 loaf). Salted butter (8oz). European cultured butter (4oz). Cream cheese (4oz). Smoked salmon (8oz). Capers (2 tbsp). Dill (1 bunch). Strawberries (1 pint). Crème fraîche (8oz). Brown sugar. French breakfast radishes (1 bunch). Flaky sea salt. Lemon zest. Flour, butter, sugar (for shortbread). Ginger ale (1L).

Roughly $115 for the perishables. The gin, Pimm's, and Lillet are pantry investments that survive a few parties.

## Faithful and modern-flexible

The Bee's Knees and Southside are non-negotiable. Both still mix in serious cocktail bars worldwide. The Clover Club substitutes pasteurized egg white from a carton for hosts whose guests are raw-egg-shy. The Lillet Spritz becomes a Verjus Spritz (verjus, soda, orange twist) for alcohol-free guests, drawn from the temperance-era mocktail set.
