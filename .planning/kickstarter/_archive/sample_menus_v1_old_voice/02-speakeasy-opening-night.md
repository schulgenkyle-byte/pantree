# Speakeasy Opening Night

**Era:** Manhattan, 1924. Below-street. Knock three times. The bar that opens at 9pm.

**Guests:** 6. Evening party, four hours, runs from 9pm to 1am.

---

## The drinks

**Hanky Panky.** Ada Coleman, American Bar at the Savoy, London, 1903. Pre-Prohibition holdover, popular in every NY speakeasy by 1923. One and a half ounces London Dry gin, one and a half ounces sweet vermouth, two dashes Fernet-Branca. Stir with ice. Strain into a coupe. Orange peel expressed and dropped.

**Sidecar.** Harry MacElhone, Harry's New York Bar, Paris, 1921. Sugar-rimmed coupe optional but era-correct. One and a half ounces cognac, three-quarter ounce orange curaçao or Cointreau, three-quarter ounce lemon juice. Shake with ice. Strain. Lemon peel.

**Mary Pickford.** Hotel Nacional Havana, attributed to a bartender named Fred Kaufman, 1922. Named for the silent film star who spent Prohibition winters in Cuba. One and a half ounces white rum, one and a half ounces pineapple juice, six dashes grenadine, two dashes maraschino liqueur. Shake hard with ice. Strain into a coupe. Maraschino cherry.

**Aviation.** Hugo Ensslin, Hotel Wallick, New York, 1916. The crème de violette is the part nobody can find in 1924, but if you can find it, the drink is itself. Two ounces gin, half ounce maraschino, quarter ounce crème de violette, half ounce lemon juice. Shake. Strain. No garnish.

**Manhattan.** The Manhattan Club, NYC, late 1870s. The drink that named itself for the city. Two ounces rye whiskey, one ounce sweet vermouth, two dashes Angostura bitters. Stir with ice. Strain into a coupe. Brandied cherry.

## The food

**Oyster crackers with smoked paprika butter.** Era-correct salt bar snack. Mix room-temperature butter with smoked paprika, sea salt, a touch of lemon zest. Smear lightly on oyster crackers. Bake at 300°F for eight minutes until just crisp.

**Country ham on biscuits.** Smithfield-style salt-cured ham, sliced paper-thin, layered into split warm buttermilk biscuits with grain mustard.

**Pickled deviled eggs.** Pre-pickle the egg whites in beet brine for 24 hours before assembly. They turn ruby red and visually carry the speakeasy table. Standard deviled-egg filling.

**Roasted nuts with rosemary and sea salt.** Walnuts and almonds, tossed in melted butter with chopped fresh rosemary and flaky salt, roasted 12 minutes at 350°F. The bowl that disappears first.

**Cheese plate.** A wedge each of aged cheddar, Stilton, and Camembert. Quince paste. Walnut bread, sliced.

**Chocolate truffles.** Plain dark-chocolate ganache rolled in cocoa powder. Two per guest. Plate at the end of the night with espresso.

---

## Host timeline

**T-24 hours:** Pickle the egg whites in beet brine. Roll the chocolate truffles.

**T-180:** Bake biscuits. Slice ham. Cool biscuits, leave covered.

**T-120:** Roast nuts. Mix paprika butter, smear and bake crackers. Assemble cheese plate. Mince rosemary.

**T-90:** Finish deviled-egg filling. Stuff egg whites. Cover and refrigerate.

**T-60:** Cut bread. Plate cheese with quince paste. Set up bar station: ice bucket, glasses, jigger, strainer, mixing glass, bar spoon, three coupes pre-chilled.

**T-30:** First Hanky Panky stirred for yourself. Test the lighting. Cue the music.

**T-0:** Guests knock. First round: Hanky Panky for the bold, Mary Pickford for the curious, Manhattan for the regulars.

## Faithful vs Modern-Flexible

The Aviation may omit the crème de violette if unavailable, becoming an "Aviation #2" (still period-correct). The pickled deviled eggs simplify to standard deviled eggs if 24-hour prep isn't possible. For alcohol-free guests, the Mary Pickford has a faithful Prohibition-era equivalent in the "Florida Cooler" (pineapple, grenadine, lime, soda) from the temperance mocktail database. All other cocktails have direct mocktail substitutes available in the app.
