# A Gatsby Affair

**Era:** West Egg, Long Island, 1925. Black-tie. The party that lasts until sunrise. The host has been seen exactly once.

**Guests:** 16 to 20. Evening into night, five hours, runs from 8pm onward.

---

## The drinks

**Gin Rickey.** Joe Rickey's invention, Shoomaker's Bar, Washington D.C., 1880s. Updated to gin from bourbon by the 1900s and made famous by *The Great Gatsby* itself ("we drank gin rickeys with the boys"). Two ounces gin, half ounce lime juice, soda water, lime wedge. Build in a highball over ice. The drink Gatsby actually mentions.

**Mint Julep.** Kentucky bourbon tradition since the 1830s. Era-appropriate for the South-fascinated wealthy of West Egg. Twelve mint leaves, half ounce simple syrup, two and a half ounces bourbon. Muddle mint with syrup in a julep cup or rocks glass. Pack with crushed ice. Pour bourbon over. Stir lightly. Top with more crushed ice. Mint bouquet.

**Pink Lady.** Jack Dunn's invention, attributed to a New York hotel bar around 1912. Two ounces gin, half ounce applejack, half ounce grenadine, half ounce lemon juice, one egg white. Dry shake. Wet shake. Strain into a coupe. The drink that signaled what kind of party it was.

**Brandy Alexander.** Troy Alexander, Rector's, New York, 1922. Built for a wedding party celebrating "Phoebe Snow," the fictional clean-clothes mascot of the Lackawanna Railroad. One ounce cognac, one ounce crème de cacao, one ounce heavy cream. Shake hard with ice. Strain into a coupe. Grated nutmeg on top.

**Champagne, simply.** Veuve Clicquot or Pol Roger if budget allows. Iced. Open on the half-hour. The drink Gatsby's parties actually ran on.

---

## The food

**Caviar service.** One ounce of American osetra or paddlefish caviar per four guests. Buckwheat blinis (warm), crème fraîche, minced shallot, chopped chive, lemon wedges, mother-of-pearl spoons. The single most period-correct expense.

**Oysters on the half-shell.** Two per guest. Lemon, mignonette (red wine vinegar, shallot, cracked pepper), horseradish, Tabasco. The 1925 American oyster bar in miniature.

**Cold poached lobster medallions.** With remoulade. Half a lobster tail per guest, pre-poached, chilled, sliced into rounds. Plate cold.

**Beef tenderloin canapés.** Rare-roasted beef tenderloin, sliced thin, on toast points with horseradish cream and watercress. Three per guest.

**Stuffed mushrooms.** White button mushrooms, stuffed with crab and breadcrumbs, baked. The hot bite that keeps people from getting too drunk too fast.

**Chocolate-dipped strawberries.** Plated at midnight. Twenty-four for sixteen guests. The dessert that becomes a kiss for someone, somewhere.

---

## Host timeline

**T-24 hours:** Order oysters and caviar for next-day pickup. Pre-poach lobster, chill. Roast beef tenderloin, chill.

**T-240:** Slice lobster medallions. Make remoulade. Slice beef paper-thin. Cover and refrigerate everything.

**T-180:** Make mignonette. Shuck or arrange to have oysters shucked at home. Bake stuffed mushrooms (par-bake, finish at T-30).

**T-120:** Toast points cut and toasted. Blinis warmed. Assemble beef canapés. Plate the caviar service on ice.

**T-90:** Set up multiple bar stations (16+ guests need at least two). Iced champagne in standing buckets. Wine fridge for the cocktail bottles. Chill all coupes.

**T-60:** Plate the chocolate-dipped strawberries (refrigerate covered). Final hair-and-tie check. Walk the rooms.

**T-30:** First Rickey for yourself on the front porch. Reheat stuffed mushrooms. Bring up the music.

**T-0:** First guests arrive. Open the first champagne audibly. The party becomes itself.

## Shopping list

Heavy cream (1 pint). Nutmeg (whole). Soda water (4 × 1L). Brut champagne (8 × 750ml or 4 × 1.5L magnums). Caviar (4-6 oz, paddlefish for value, osetra for the gesture). Buckwheat for blinis. Crème fraîche. Shallots. Chives. Mother-of-pearl spoons (rentable). Oysters (32-40). Mignonette ingredients. Tabasco. Lobster tails (8). Remoulade ingredients. Beef tenderloin (1.5 lbs). Watercress. Horseradish. White button mushrooms (24). Crabmeat (8oz). Breadcrumbs. Strawberries (24 large). Dark chocolate (8oz). Toast points / baguette (3 loaves).

Approximate spend: $625 for 16 guests, excluding spirits. This is the most expensive menu in the set. The era's parties were not cheap.

## Faithful vs Modern-Flexible

The caviar may scale to paddlefish ($60/oz) instead of osetra ($150/oz) without anyone of taste objecting. Oysters can be replaced with pickled shrimp for guests with shellfish reservations. Lobster medallions become shrimp cocktail for tighter budgets. The Brandy Alexander has a faithful non-alcoholic substitute in the in-app database (Cocoa Cream Float, c. 1928). For vegetarian guests, replace lobster and beef canapés with mushroom pâté on toast and tomato-jam crostini, both period-correct hotel-bar standards.
