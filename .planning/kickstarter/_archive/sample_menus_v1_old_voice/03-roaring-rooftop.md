# The Roaring Rooftop

**Era:** Manhattan, August 1926. The hotel rooftop bar that operates "for residents only" but takes anyone in evening dress.

**Guests:** 12. Evening party, three hours, runs from 7pm to 10pm.

---

## The drinks

**French 75.** Harry MacElhone, Harry's New York Bar, Paris, 1915. Named for the French 75mm field gun. One ounce gin, half ounce lemon juice, half ounce simple syrup, three ounces champagne. Shake gin, lemon, syrup with ice. Strain into a flute. Top with cold champagne. Long lemon peel.

**White Lady.** Harry MacElhone again, 1929 reformulation at Harry's New York Bar. One and a half ounces gin, three-quarter ounce Cointreau, three-quarter ounce lemon juice, one egg white. Dry shake. Wet shake. Strain into a coupe.

**Boulevardier.** Erskine Gwynne, Harry's New York Bar, 1927. Gwynne edited an expatriate Paris magazine called *Boulevardier*. One and a half ounces bourbon, one ounce Campari, one ounce sweet vermouth. Stir. Strain into a coupe. Orange peel.

**Last Word.** Detroit Athletic Club, circa 1916. Forgotten for fifty years and rediscovered in 2003. Three-quarter ounce gin, three-quarter ounce green Chartreuse, three-quarter ounce maraschino, three-quarter ounce lime juice. Shake with ice. Strain into a coupe.

**Champagne Cocktail.** Pre-Prohibition holdover, standardized by Jerry Thomas in 1862. One sugar cube saturated with Angostura bitters, dropped into a flute, filled with cold champagne. Lemon peel. The drink for guests who don't want to commit to a cocktail.

---

## The food

**Gougères.** Cheese choux puffs. Aged Gruyère, black pepper, baked the morning of. Twelve per twelve guests, plus extras for the inevitable second pass.

**Smoked trout on pumpernickel.** Pumpernickel rounds, horseradish cream, smoked trout, dill, lemon zest.

**Watermelon and feta on toothpicks.** A 1920s American hotel-bar invention. Watermelon cubes with crumbled feta and a mint leaf, on a toothpick. Anachronistically simple, era-appropriate to within a decade.

**Olives and almonds.** Marcona almonds with sea salt. Castelvetrano olives. Manzanilla olives. One small bowl of each. Refill twice during the party.

**Beef carpaccio crostini.** Thin-sliced raw eye-of-round, capers, shaved Parmesan, lemon, olive oil, on toasted baguette. Make the morning of, plate just before guests arrive.

**Macarons.** Store-bought is fine. The era didn't have macarons in the U.S. yet (they hit American shores in the 1930s), but the rooftop sensibility tolerates the slight anachronism for the visual.

---

## Host timeline

**T-180:** Bake gougères. Toast baguette slices for the carpaccio. Slice trout.

**T-120:** Slice carpaccio (or have the butcher do it). Plate. Cover and refrigerate.

**T-90:** Cube watermelon. Crumble feta. Pluck mint. Assemble watermelon picks. Refrigerate.

**T-60:** Chill champagne, gin, glassware. Set up bar station outdoors. Set the music. Cut lemon peels.

**T-30:** Pour yourself a Champagne Cocktail. Make sure the bar setup is wind-stable. Light the candles.

**T-0:** Guests arrive in evening dress. The first French 75 is the photograph everyone takes.

## Faithful vs Modern-Flexible

The Last Word is the only drink with no easy non-alcoholic substitute. For alcohol-free guests, the in-app temperance database offers the "Cinderella" (orange + lemon + pineapple + grenadine + ginger ale, served in a flute), a 1923 American Hotel Bar standard. The beef carpaccio becomes a roasted-beet carpaccio for vegetarian hosts (the visual carries). Macarons can be omitted entirely for budget reasons; the era won't notice.
