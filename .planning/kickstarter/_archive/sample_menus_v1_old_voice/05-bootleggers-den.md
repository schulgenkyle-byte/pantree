# Bootlegger's Den

**Era:** Chicago, 1929. A converted basement. Wood plank floor. Two musicians in the corner. Eight chairs.

**Guests:** 8. Late evening, four hours, runs from 10pm to 2am.

---

## The drinks

**Old Fashioned.** Pendennis Club, Louisville, 1880s. The drink that defined the word "cocktail." One sugar cube, three dashes Angostura bitters, one orange peel, one teaspoon water. Muddle in a rocks glass. Add two ounces rye whiskey. Add one large ice cube. Stir.

**Brooklyn.** Jack Grohusko, *Jack's Manual*, 1908. Two ounces rye, one ounce dry vermouth, quarter ounce maraschino, quarter ounce Amer Picon (or Bigallet China-China as substitute). Stir. Strain into a coupe. The drink that made New York's other borough.

**Vieux Carré.** Walter Bergeron, Carousel Bar at the Hotel Monteleone, New Orleans, 1937. Slightly outside Prohibition but worth the inclusion for the historical lineage. One ounce rye, one ounce cognac, one ounce sweet vermouth, half teaspoon Bénédictine, two dashes Peychaud's bitters, two dashes Angostura. Stir. Strain over fresh ice in a rocks glass. Lemon peel.

**Bramble.** Modern recreation of a 1920s style, popularized by Dick Bradsell in 1980s London but based on lost American basement-bar templates. One and a half ounces gin, three-quarter ounce lemon juice, half ounce simple syrup. Shake with ice. Pour into a rocks glass over crushed ice. Drizzle a quarter ounce of crème de mûre (blackberry liqueur) over the top. Lemon wedge, fresh blackberry.

**Sazerac.** Antoine Amédée Peychaud, New Orleans, 1850s. Pre-Prohibition holdover, kept alive in Chicago basement bars by way of New Orleans expatriates. Rinse a rocks glass with absinthe (or Herbsaint), discard the absinthe. In a separate mixing glass: one sugar cube, three dashes Peychaud's bitters, two ounces rye whiskey, ice. Stir. Strain into the absinthe-rinsed glass. Lemon peel expressed and discarded.

## The food

**Country pâté on bread.** Coarse pork pâté with pistachios and brandy, served at room temperature on grilled country bread. The basement bar standard.

**Pickled eggs.** Whole hard-boiled eggs in beet brine, sliced into quarters. The bar food of every illicit American basement room in 1929.

**Cured sausage and cheese.** A board with thinly sliced salami, soppressata, and dry cheddar. Whole-grain mustard. Cornichons. Walnut bread.

**Slow-cooked pork shoulder, pulled.** Served warm on small biscuits with mustard. The hot food at midnight.

**Hot peanuts.** Boiled and salted, served warm in a paper-lined bowl. The bowl that keeps people drinking through the slow hour.

**Apple pie.** One pie. Eight slices. Plated with a small wedge of sharp cheddar on the side, Wisconsin-style. The dessert that ends the night.

## Host timeline

**T-24 hours:** Brine pickled eggs. Slow-cook pork shoulder (oven, 6 hours at 275°F, cool overnight). Bake apple pie. Boil and salt peanuts.

**T-180:** Pull the pork. Reheat gently in the oven covered. Cut country bread.

**T-120:** Slice salami and soppressata. Assemble the cheese board. Make biscuits or warm pre-made.

**T-90:** Pour the pâté into a serving dish. Slice pickled eggs into quarters. Plate.

**T-60:** Set up the bar in the basement corner. One rocks glass per guest, one coupe per guest. Polished. Ice. Crushed ice (separate). Lemon peels cut. Sugar cubes ready.

**T-30:** Pour yourself an Old Fashioned. Light the candles. Cue the musicians or the playlist.

**T-0:** Guests descend. The first round is Old Fashioneds and Brooklyns. The Sazerac is the second round, made theatrically.

## Music

Chicago jazz and early blues. King Oliver's Creole Jazz Band, Louis Armstrong's Hot Five sides, Ma Rainey's Paramount recordings. Live piano if a friend can play. Otherwise the in-app "Chicago Basement, 1929" playlist auto-loads.

## Faithful vs Modern-Flexible

The Sazerac is the drink that historically requires the most explanation. Hosts who want simplicity may swap it for a second round of Old Fashioneds. The Vieux Carré is technically 1937 and may be flagged in the in-app "Strict-Faithful" filter; the menu defaults to its inclusion because Chicago basement-bar lineage carries it. Vegetarian guests get a mushroom pâté and roasted-vegetable biscuits instead of pork shoulder, both period-correct American bar standards.
