Pre-Prohibition cocktails. From what's in your fridge.

Every cocktail menu in every craft bar in America in 2026 is a footnote on a $1 book published during the Civil War.

That book is How to Mix Drinks, or The Bon Vivant's Companion. Jerry Thomas, 1862. Worked the Occidental in San Francisco, the Metropolitan in New York. Wrote down recipes that had previously lived in oral tradition between bartenders. The first American bartender's manual that survived. The thread starts there.

Engel followed in 1878. Johnson in 1882. Boothby in 1908. Ensslin in 1916. Five primary sources. Not fifty. Five.

Then Prohibition started. The thread stopped for thirteen years.

I built Speakeater to keep it going.

What's already built

I photographed the manuscripts. I sourced 2,846 cocktails from the manuscripts. Original text preserved alongside the modernized recipe. Original typography preserved, including the long-s that reads as "f" in the 1916 Ensslin. The app shows you the facsimile page next to your phone. That mode is called Bootlegger.

Beyond those: 2,848 pre-Prohibition cocktails sourced from the period. 6,539 drinks total counting modern revival classics and the 1,361-strong temperance mocktail set. 23,743 food recipes that get ranked by what you already own when you photograph the fridge.

The app exists. I will send you an Internal Testing build the week this campaign closes, at $25 or above.

What this Kickstarter funds

Curate a Party. Fifty menus written by hand from the primary sources. Plus Mystery Nights — five murder-mystery and role-play menus released at launch, with twenty-five more as a stretch.

Each menu names the bar, the bartender, the year. Each cocktail in each menu cites its first appearance in print. Each food pairing is what the era's hotel bars actually served.

The Bee's Knees room at the Ritz Paris, 1929. Speakeasy opening night, Manhattan 1924. A Long Island estate in 1928. The basement bar in Chicago, 1929. Forty-five more like them.

Then Mystery Nights. Five at launch. Murder at the Algonquin (Round Table, 1924). The Bootlegger's Wife (Chicago basement, 1929). Last Toast at the Ritz (Paris, 1925). Heir to the Pendennis (Louisville Derby Eve, 1923). The Vanishing Socialite (West Egg, 1926). Each one a three-hour evening with cast, hook, host playbook, and reveal. Twenty-five more unlock at $50,000.

I will write all of them by hand. Same way I worked through the 2,846 cocktails. Slow. Not glamorous. The part you cannot fake.

The campaign funds the build: the menu authoring tool that lets me write efficiently, the PDF generator that ships each menu as a printable host kit, and six months of solo-dev runway so the app launches on Play Store with Curate-a-Party and Mystery Nights live on day one.
