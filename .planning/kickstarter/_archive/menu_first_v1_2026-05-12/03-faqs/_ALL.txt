[FAQ 2]

QUESTION:
Why Prohibition?

ANSWER:
Because the thread stops there. 1862 to 1923 is when the American bartender's manuscripts were written. 1920 to 1933 erased the bars and most of the bartenders. The craft revival of the late 1990s and 2000s was, in important respects, a project of returning to that thread and continuing it. Speakeater's job is to make the source material legible to a person at home tonight.

[FAQ 3]

QUESTION:
What is Curate a Party, mechanically?

ANSWER:
A menu is five drinks, six small plates, a host timeline, a shopping list, and a music note. Each menu is dated, sourced, and named for the bar that inspired it. You download one to your phone. You shop. You host.

Every menu ships in two registers. Faithful uses the period ingredients exactly. Modern-flexible offers substitutions for vegetarian, gluten-free, or alcohol-free hosts. The mocktail set covers the alcohol-free swaps with real temperance-era recipes.

[FAQ 6]

QUESTION:
Why does Ensslin show up as "1916 (expanded 1917)"?

ANSWER:
Because Hugo Ensslin published Recipes for Mixed Drinks in 1916 and expanded it the next year. The expanded edition contains the Aviation, which lost its color for almost a century when the recipe got reprinted without the crème de violette.

[FAQ 7]

QUESTION:
Is this an AI-generated app?

ANSWER:
No. The recipes are not generated. The cocktails are transcribed from primary-source manuscripts by hand. The food recipes are licensed, edited, and ranked. The only AI involvement is the vision OCR that reads your fridge and the matching engine that ranks recipes by ingredient overlap. The recipes themselves are real.
