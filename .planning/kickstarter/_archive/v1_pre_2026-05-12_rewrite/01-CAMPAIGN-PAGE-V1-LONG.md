# A murder mystery in your kitchen. Five phones. One bartender's manual from 1862.

## Speakeater is a multiplayer party game built on the cocktail-history record from 1862 to 1923.

Eight guests at the table. Each one a phone in their hand. Each phone running Speakeater, joined to the same game via a four-letter code. Each guest a character. Each guest a secret. The murder, the cast, the cocktails, and the food are all pulled from primary sources you can verify on Internet Archive.

The drinks are real. The food is real. The mystery is the part you play.

I built this so a person at home tonight can host an evening that does what a $200-a-head murder mystery night does, with primary-source cocktail history layered underneath it, for the price of a single bottle.

## How a Mystery Night works

You and seven guests are at the dinner table. The food is in front of you. The cocktails are mixed.

You tap "Host a Game" in the Speakeater app. A four-letter code appears on your phone. RYAS. Or LION. Or TANG. The other seven guests open Speakeater, tap "Join a Game," and enter the code. Their phones light up with their character. Name. Role. Secret only they know.

You tap Begin.

For the next three hours, the game runs across all eight phones at once. Some beats are broadcast. Everyone sees them. Some beats are private. Only one player's phone lights up with a twist, a clue, a multiple-choice action. What that player chooses changes what other players see five minutes later. Each player drives part of the story. The reveal lands at the last cocktail.

The five Mystery Nights ready at launch:

- **Murder at the Algonquin.** Manhattan, 1924. The Round Table set. The theatre critic is dead.
- **The Bootlegger's Wife.** Chicago, 1929. A basement bar after a raid. Someone tipped the feds.
- **Last Toast at the Ritz.** Paris, 1925. Frank Meier's bar at closing. The wealthy patron just collapsed.
- **Heir to the Pendennis.** Louisville, 1923. Derby Eve. The old colonel's will reading is tomorrow. He won't see it.
- **The Vanishing Socialite.** West Egg, 1926. A young woman left the ballroom at midnight. She never made it home.

Each game ships with five characters, a hook, multiple-choice actions, plot twists, and the reveal.

## FAQ

**Is the app actually working?**

Yes. Free tier on Android via Google Play from June 10, 2026. Backers at $19 and above get the Internal Testing build the week this campaign closes. The five Mystery Nights are interactive multiplayer games from day one.

**How does the multiplayer game work, mechanically?**

Eight phones at a dinner. One host opens a game in Speakeater and gets a four-letter code. The other guests open Speakeater on their own phones, tap "Join a Game," type the code. Each phone gets a private character: name, role, the one secret only they know. The host taps Begin. Over the next three hours, the game runs across all eight phones via WebSocket. Some beats are broadcast (everyone sees them). Some beats are private (only one phone). Multiple-choice actions cascade. What one player picks changes what others see five minutes later. The reveal lands at the last cocktail.

**Why Prohibition?**

Because the thread stops there. 1862 to 1923 is when the American bartender's manuscripts were written. 1920 to 1933 erased the bars and most of the bartenders. The craft revival of the late 1990s and 2000s was, in important respects, a project of returning to that thread and continuing it. Speakeater's job is to make the source material legible to a person at home tonight.

**What is Curate-a-Party, mechanically?**

A menu is five drinks, six small plates, a host timeline, a shopping list, and a music note. Each menu is dated, sourced, and named for the bar that inspired it. Every Mystery Night ships with one of these menus baked in. Beyond the five Mystery Nights, fifty additional standalone Curate-a-Party menus ship across the next twelve months.

**What happens to my games and menus if Speakeater shuts down?**

Every menu exports as a PDF. Every Mystery Night exports as a printable host-and-player kit. If the servers die, your saved games survive on your phone. Founder Rate subscriptions are honored for the operational lifetime of the app. Backers at the $30 tier or above lock the $30 a year rate forever, grandfathered as long as the subscription stays active. If the app shuts down, all Pro features unlock for everyone via the final update.

**Do I need everyone to have an iPhone or Android?**

Android via Google Play from launch. iOS late summer 2026. Mystery Nights need each guest to have the app on their phone. A web join page (no app required) ships alongside the native app for guests who can't or won't install. They get the same character card and the same beats, via the browser.

**Are you a historian?**

No. I am a solo software developer who got too into the subject to stay quiet. I am wrong somewhere. The Codex methodology page at speakeater.com/codex/methodology documents what I know and what I do not. If you find an error, email me. I read every one.

**Is this an AI-generated app?**

No. The cocktail recipes are transcribed from primary-source manuscripts by hand. The food recipes are licensed, edited, and ranked. The Mystery Night writing is mine. The only AI involvement is the vision OCR that reads your fridge, the matching engine that ranks recipes by ingredient overlap, and the optional Video Clue Pack stretch goal which uses AI video generation tools to produce period-styled twist clips. The recipes, the games, and the cocktail history themselves are real.
