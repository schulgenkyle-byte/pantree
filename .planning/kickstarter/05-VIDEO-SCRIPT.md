# Hero Video — 90 seconds

The Kickstarter trailer. The one video that has to do all the heavy lifting on the campaign page above the fold.

Two production paths:
- **Path A:** Live shoot. Kyle on camera in his actual kitchen + a small speakeasy-styled second location (rentable bar, basement, or friend's home). One day of shooting, half-day of editing. Budget: $500-1500 depending on whether you DIY or hire a one-person crew.
- **Path B:** Speakeater Studio Remotion render. All motion graphics + voiceover. Reuses the brand pack and the existing Remotion compositions. Budget: zero dollars, your own time.

Path B is the realistic recommendation for a $15k campaign. Path A is better if you can afford it.

The script below is written so it works for either path. The shot column changes; the voiceover and supers don't.

---

## Shooting structure (90 seconds total)

### Cold open: 0:00 - 0:08 (8 seconds)

**Visual (Path A):** Tight on the inside of a fridge. Hand opens the door. The light comes on. Wilted herbs. A half-block of cheese. Three eggs.

**Visual (Path B):** Animated 2D fridge interior in the editorial dark palette. Items fade in one at a time. Type label appears under each: "spinach (4 days)" / "eggs (12)" / "cheddar (8oz)."

**Voiceover (founder voice, Kyle):**
> Most cooking apps want to teach you to cook.

**Super (text on screen, lower third):** (none)

---

### The pain: 0:08 - 0:20 (12 seconds)

**Visual (Path A):** Camera pulls back. Kyle standing in his kitchen, looking at his phone, looking at the fridge, looking back at the phone. Tired. End-of-day.

**Visual (Path B):** Animated phone screen showing a generic cooking-app feed of glossy recipes. Cursor scrolls past them. Recipes get checked against "ingredients you have" — all show red X marks.

**Voiceover:**
> You don't have time. You have ingredients. A tired hour between work and dinner. And a vague sense you should be using what's already in the icebox before it spoils.

---

### The product: 0:20 - 0:42 (22 seconds)

**Visual (Path A):** Kyle raises phone, points camera at open fridge. Camera flash. Phone screen shows Speakeater scan results: ingredients identified, scrolling list with green checkmarks. Then a recipe deck swipes by, one card landing with "94% match: Spinach and feta frittata."

**Visual (Path B):** Animated phone. Camera shutter sound. Pantry items populate. Recipe cards swipe up from below, each labeled with a match percentage. One settles in the center: "94% match."

**Voiceover:**
> I built Speakeater for that hour. You photograph your fridge. The app tells you what's in there. It hands you a stack of recipes that match what you have, ranked by how many ingredients you already own. Tap one. Cook. Done.

**Super (lower third):** Speakeater · the cooking app that reads your fridge

---

### The twist: 0:42 - 0:55 (13 seconds)

**Audio:** Music shifts. The clean modern background score gives way to a 1925 stride piano stinger.

**Visual (Path A):** Cut to Kyle pouring a cocktail in a low-light bar setting. Coupe glass, ice in a mixing pitcher. The app on the counter shows a Hanky Panky recipe. Wide on the cocktail being strained.

**Visual (Path B):** Sepia filter sweeps across the screen. The app's Mixology tab flips into Bootlegger mode. A coupe glass animates into focus with rotating cocktail names underneath: "Hanky Panky · 1903 · Savoy" / "Sidecar · 1921 · Paris" / "Bee's Knees · 1929 · Ritz."

**Voiceover:**
> The cocktail half is the part nobody else does. Sixteen hundred drinks. Every one of them sourced from a specific bar, a specific bartender, a specific year.

---

### Curate a Party: 0:55 - 1:15 (20 seconds)

**Visual (Path A):** Phone screen tilts up to camera. Curate-a-Party menu detail: "The Bee's Knees Garden Party — Long Island, 1928." Hero image of the menu. The five cocktail names scroll past. Then six food names. Then a host timeline.

**Visual (Path B):** Animated menu card flips open. The menu's structure animates in section by section: title, cocktails (5 stack from bottom), food (6 stack from bottom), timeline (clock animation), shopping list (checkmark scroll).

**Voiceover:**
> This Kickstarter funds one new feature. Curate a Party. Fifty Prohibition-era menus, written by hand. Five drinks, six small plates, a host timeline, a shopping list. Everything you need to throw a 1925 speakeasy night in a 2026 apartment.

---

### Close: 1:15 - 1:30 (15 seconds)

**Visual (Path A):** Wide shot. Kyle holding the phone, party in soft focus behind him.

**Visual (Path B):** Final title card. Logo. Funding goal counter. URL.

**Voiceover:**
> I'm Kyle. I've been building this alone for fourteen months. If you're the kind of person who's been waiting for an app that takes the era seriously, I'd love to have you in the first hundred backers.

**Super (full screen, end card):**
> Speakeater on Kickstarter
> Now live · 30 days
> kickstarter.com/projects/speakeater

## Production notes

**Music licensing:** Use a Royalty-free 1920s jazz track. Bensound and Epidemic Sound both have era-appropriate options under $20/year. Never use uncleared archival recordings (those Eubie Blake records are in copyright until 2034).

**Voiceover:** Founder-singular voice means Kyle records this himself. Use a USB cardioid mic in a closet draped with blankets. Audacity to clean up. The 1920s radio-announcer style is a temptation to over-style; resist. Read the script straight. The contrast of plain modern voice over period-correct visuals is the editorial point.

**Captioning:** Burn in subtitles. Auto-played KS videos run muted on mobile by default. Caption styling: Source Serif 4 (the app's display font), 24pt, ink-on-paper colors (Ink #F4ECD9 on Paper #020203).

**Aspect ratios:** Render in 16:9 master for the KS embed. Also export 9:16 vertical for TikTok/Reels/Shorts cross-posting, with the supers re-positioned.

**Length discipline:** 90 seconds is the upper bound. Kickstarter's own data shows campaign videos under 2 minutes outperform longer videos by 65% in completion rate. Cut tight.

## B-roll shot list (for Path A)

If you go live-action, capture these as fallback / for the campaign update videos later:

1. Pour shot: jigger pouring into coupe, slow motion
2. Lemon peel express over a drink, slow motion
3. Honey syrup dripping into a glass
4. Hand shaking a cocktail shaker
5. Strain into coupe, slow motion
6. Wide on the finished cocktail with garnish
7. Phone-in-hand showing the Bootlegger sepia mode
8. Phone-on-counter showing the Modern dark mode
9. Fridge open, hand reaching for an ingredient
10. The five sample menu cards on a table, arranged like a fan
11. Print booklet (mock-up if not yet produced) being held open
12. Old cocktail book open on a table next to a tablet showing the app

Capture all 12 in one shooting day. You'll re-use these across 30 days of social posts, the campaign updates, and the launch trailer.
